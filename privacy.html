Privacy policy

Last updated 18 November 2026. This policy covers the Airwave app for Android (package uk.jj.airwave) and this website. Airwave is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Airwave has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • Your favourites, your own stations, your podcasts, your listening history and your settings are kept on your phone.
  • To play a station or a podcast, your phone connects straight to that station or podcast. To show listings, it asks two public directories. Those services see what any website sees: your internet address and what was asked for. They are listed below.
  • Your location is used only if you tap Stations near me. It stays on the phone.

What stays on your phone

Airwave keeps the following in its private storage:

  • Your favourites and folders, the stations you played recently, and the stations and categories you have hidden.
  • The stations and podcasts you added yourself, and your saved filters.
  • The list of songs played, if you leave that switched on. It has a Clear button.
  • Your place in each podcast episode, your queue, and any episodes you downloaded.
  • Your alarm, your settings, and a count of the data Airwave has used.
  • A copy of the station directory, so that the lists open without waiting.
  • While a station plays with pause and rewind switched on, the last few minutes of that station. This is deleted when you stop or change station.

Unless you switch it off, Airwave also keeps one backup copy of your favourites, stations, podcasts and settings in the phone's Documents folder, in a folder called Airwave. That file stays there if you uninstall the app, so that you can restore from it. If your phone's own backup to your Google account is on, Android may include Airwave's settings and favourites in it, as it does for most apps.

Services the app contacts

Airwave has no server of its own. Your phone connects directly to the services below. Each sees your internet address and the technical details any web server sees, and handles them under its own privacy policy. None of them is sent your name, your favourites or your history, because Airwave does not have a way to send them.

A station or podcast you add yourself is contacted in the same way as any other. Airwave allows plain, unencrypted connections, because many radio stations still use them; on such a connection your network provider can see which station you are playing.

Stations and podcasts

Airwave does not run, host or re-broadcast any station or podcast. What you hear comes straight from the broadcaster or publisher, who is responsible for it and for any adverts or listener counting in it.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Favourites, your own stations, podcasts, saved filters and hidden stations can each be removed where they are listed.
  • The list of songs played has a Clear button, and can be switched off in Settings.
  • Downloaded episodes can be deleted one at a time from the Downloads list.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling Airwave deletes everything it holds, apart from the backup copy.
  • The backup copy is an ordinary file, airwave-backup.json, in Documents under Airwave. Delete it there when you no longer want it. A backup file you saved yourself is wherever you put it.
  • Nothing is held by the developer, so there is nothing further to delete.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Airwave holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. What Airwave stores is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. For data held by a station, a podcast host or one of the services listed, contact that service. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

What Airwave stores is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. The backup copy in Documents can be read by you and by apps you have given access to your files; it holds station and podcast addresses and settings, and no passwords. Connections to the directories and to GitHub are encrypted. Connections to stations are encrypted where the station offers it.

No software is free of faults. If you find anything that does not match this policy, please report it.

This website

This site sets no cookies and has no analytics or advertising. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and the app file is downloaded from GitHub Releases. GitHub, as the host, receives the technical details any web server sees, such as your IP address and browser type, and handles them under the GitHub privacy statement. The developer does not receive them.

Children

Airwave is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone. Radio stations and podcasts carry all kinds of material, some of it unsuitable for children. A parent or guardian should decide whether a child uses it.
